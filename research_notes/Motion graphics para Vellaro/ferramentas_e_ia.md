# Ferramentas e IA para motion graphics, animação 3D de produto e vídeo curto (Vellaro Design, 2025–2026)

Research date: 2026-10-31. Scope: software and AI services for Reels/Stories, product and process animations, 3D renders of custom furniture and website motion assets. The client is a small Brazilian bespoke luxury furniture workshop with a non-technical team that works with an AI coding assistant.

Source-quality caveat (applies throughout): most pricing figures found in this round come from third-party comparison and SEO blogs dated 2026, not vendor pages. Many disagree with each other, and the disagreements are flagged inline. Vendor pages at runwayml.com, support.tiktok.com and journal.everypixel.com could not be fetched: they were blocked by the session's network allowlist. Confirm every price on the official page, and in BRL where possible, before budgeting.

## 1. Traditional and modern motion tools (After Effects/Premiere, Cavalry, Blender, Cinema 4D, Spline, Rive, Jitter, Canva/CapCut, DaVinci Resolve/Fusion): pricing, strengths, learning curve

### Takeaway
For a two- or three-person luxury workshop, the strongest value stack in 2026 is the free tier of DaVinci Resolve (editing, color grading and Fusion compositing, no watermark), the now-free Cavalry (2D procedural motion), and the free Blender (3D product renders). After Effects is still the industry standard for frame-precise motion and compositing, but it costs about US$21–31/month (about R$90–135/month in Brazil, not verified with Adobe) and has a steep learning curve. Jitter and Rive are the cheap, fast options for web and UI motion.

- Cavalry going free (if confirmed) makes it the strongest no-cost option for motion typography and logo animation. Because Canva now owns it, Canva integration may follow.
- After Effects only makes sense if Vellaro hires a freelance motion designer who already uses it. For a non-technical team, the learning curve and monthly cost are hard to justify against Resolve, Cavalry and Canva.
- Learning-curve ranking from the sources' positioning and general knowledge (not quantitatively sourced), easiest to hardest: Canva/CapCut < Jitter < Spline/Rive < Cavalry < Resolve/Fusion < After Effects < Cinema 4D < Blender.

## 2. AI video, AI 3D and AI voice/music tools: pricing, quality, limitations and commercial-use licensing

### Takeaway
The AI video market consolidated sharply in 2026. OpenAI discontinued Sora: the app closed on 26 Apr 2026 and the API on 24 Sep 2026. The practical options for Brazil are now:
- Google Veo 3.1 through Flow and the Google AI plans, priced in BRL: R$24,99 for AI Plus and R$96,99/month for AI Pro;
- Runway Gen-4.5, whose docs say Runway does not claim ownership of outputs or restrict commercial use;
- Kling 3.0, cheap but with a vaguer "usage right" rather than ownership;
- Luma Ray3.x, Hailuo, Higgsfield and Pika;
- Adobe Firefly, the most "commercially safe", but its indemnity covers only Adobe's own models and only on eligible enterprise plans.

Free tiers almost always prohibit commercial use. Brand-safe music is a minefield: ElevenLabs Music has the cleanest licensing, Suno grants "commercial rights" on paid plans but is still in litigation, and Udio downloads are disabled.

## 3. Code-based or programmatic video (Remotion, Motion Canvas, HyperFrames, Lottie/dotLottie) when an AI assistant builds the video from code

### Takeaway
Remotion (React) is free for commercial use by organizations of up to three people, so Vellaro currently qualifies. Above that, licensing costs US$25 per seat per month for anyone writing Remotion code, including through agentic AI tools. HeyGen's HyperFrames (HTML/CSS to MP4, built for AI agents, Apache 2.0) has no license fee at any company size. Both suit templated, on-brand motion typography over real footage that an AI coding assistant can write.

### Cited Findings
- Remotion is free for individuals and organizations of up to three people, including commercial use. Four or more people need a Company License. The trigger is headcount, not revenue, and contractors count toward team size. — [Remotion License FAQ](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/faq); [snapcn](https://snapcn.dev/remotion-license-pricing)
- Remotion Company License terms:
  - Creators: US$25 per seat per month, for "each person who writes Remotion code themselves or using agentic coding tools";
  - Automators: US$0.01 per render with a US$100/month minimum;
  - Enterprise: same as Company, with a US$500 minimum.
  
  Some third-party sites cite a US$1.000 annual minimum. — [Wireflow](https://www.wireflow.ai/blog/remotion-pricing); [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/remotion/pricing); [Remotion License FAQ](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/faq)
- HyperFrames (HeyGen) is an open framework that renders HTML, CSS and seekable animations into MP4. It is "built for agents" and licensed under Apache 2.0, with no per-render fees or commercial thresholds. It is distinct from HeyGen's avatar product and is inspired by Remotion but uses HTML instead of React. One source contradictorily calls it "source-available, not open source", while the GitHub repo states Apache 2.0. — [GitHub heygen-com/hyperframes](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes); [The Menon Lab](https://themenonlab.blog/blog/heygen-hyperframes-video-as-code); [note.com comparison](https://note.com/ai_video_creator/n/nc370788a3c36?hl=en)
- Jitter's free plan exports watermarked Lottie, and Pro removes the watermark. After Effects has no native Lottie export. — [Art of Styleframe](https://artofstyleframe.com/blog/jitter-vs-after-effects-motion-design/); [SVGator](https://www.svgator.com/blog/best-after-effects-alternatives/)

### Inferences
- These options fit a non-technical team with an AI coding assistant well. The assistant can produce reusable templates in code, such as a branded intro, a piece name with wood species and dimensions, a "from sketch to piece" timeline, or a carousel-to-Reel. The team then only swaps clips and text. Output is deterministic, so brand typography and colors stay pixel-consistent, unlike generative AI video.
- HyperFrames is the safer long-term license if the team or its contractors grow beyond three people. Remotion has the larger ecosystem and documentation.
- For website motion assets, Lottie/dotLottie (from Jitter, Cavalry or LottieFiles) or Rive files are lighter than MP4 and can be embedded by the coding assistant.

### Gaps
- Motion Canvas licensing and status in 2026: not researched. It is believed to be MIT-licensed and TypeScript-based, but this is unverified.
- dotLottie and LottieFiles pricing and licensing: not retrieved.
- No source on render-time and hardware requirements for these tools on a typical small-business laptop.

## 4. Realistic workflows for premium furniture videos, and risks (AI artifacts, misrepresentation, Meta/TikTok AI labeling)

### Takeaway
The lowest-risk, most "luxury-authentic" path is real phone footage of the workshop and finished pieces, graded in Resolve and overlaid with restrained motion typography (Cavalry, Canva, or Remotion/HyperFrames written by the AI assistant). Blender renders suit custom pieces that don't exist yet, as long as they are clearly presented as renders. AI image-to-video from real product photos is best limited to ambient camera moves and B-roll. Any visible AI-generated or AI-altered realistic content must be disclosed: Meta applies "AI info" labels via C2PA/IPTC metadata or self-disclosure, and TikTok requires an AI-generated toggle and reads C2PA credentials.

### Cited Findings
- Meta applies an "AI info" label to AI-generated or AI-modified content on Facebook, Instagram and Threads. Detection relies on C2PA and IPTC metadata or creator self-disclosure. The label was renamed from "Made with AI" on 1 July 2024. Since 12 Sep 2024, content that was only edited with AI has the label moved into the post menu. — [Postora](https://www.postora.org/blog/ai-generated-content-labels-social-media-2026); [Makeinfluence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/content-credentials-c2pa-how-platforms-label-ai-assisted-content); [Frameos](https://frameos.studio/blog/ai-content-disclosure-labels)
- Meta ads with AI-generated or significantly AI-edited content reportedly need an "AI info" disclosure. — [Everything-PR](https://everything-pr.com/ai-generated-content-disclosure-platform-by-platform-rules)
- TikTok has an AI-generated content toggle at upload and reads C2PA Content Credentials to auto-label. Dates conflict: 9 May 2024 (first video platform to implement C2PA) versus January 2025. For ads, an auto-applied label reportedly cannot be removed by the advertiser. ### Inferences
Suggested workflows for Vellaro, in order of realism and risk:

1. **Phone footage plus motion typography (core weekly content).** Record 4K on a phone: wood grain, hand tools, joinery, finishing, the final piece in situ. Grade and edit in free DaVinci Resolve. Add titles and a logo sting from Cavalry or Canva, or from templates the AI assistant builds in Remotion/HyperFrames. Add licensed music from ElevenLabs or the platforms' in-app libraries. Cost is near zero and nothing needs an AI label. This is the most authentic fit for a bespoke luxury brand.
2. **Blender or 3D product renders (pre-sale proposals, website hero loops).** Build the client's piece in Blender, or from a CAD model if the workshop has one. Render turntables and material variants. This is the best way to show a piece before it is built. Label it as a "render" or "projeto" so clients don't take it for a finished piece. The learning curve is steep, so outsource to a freelancer or keep it simple. Spline is a lighter in-browser option for interactive web 3D (pricing not verified).
3. **AI image-to-video from real product photos (selective B-roll).** Use Veo 3.1 through Google AI Pro (BRL billing), Runway or Kling to add slow push-ins, light sweeps or ambient scenes around a real photo. The risks:
   - the AI can subtly change proportions, grain, hardware or upholstery, which misrepresents a made-to-order product (consumer-protection exposure under Brazil's CDC; inference, not researched here);
   - generated text and logos are unreliable;
   - outputs must carry the AI label on Meta and TikTok;
   - SynthID or C2PA can trigger auto-labels even if the brand doesn't self-disclose.
   
   Mitigations: keep clips short, review every frame against the real piece, never use AI to depict finishes or dimensions the product doesn't have, and composite real typography afterwards rather than generating it.
4. **Avoid** fully text-to-video "fake" showroom or lifestyle scenes presented as real work. The reputational risk is high for a craft brand whose value proposition is authenticity.

Additional notes:
- Free tiers of Kling, Tripo and most others prohibit commercial use or require attribution (Meshy CC BY). Vellaro should budget at least one paid tier for any AI tool whose output appears in ads or on the website.
